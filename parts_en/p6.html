Part VI

Technical Bootcamp and final evaluation

An intensive week to go deeper, a 48-hour hackathon to prove it and an evaluation that separates completing, mastering and defending.

Section 21Technical Bootcamp and advanced specialization

The Technical Bootcamp is the advanced development phase that follows The AI Challenge and final selection. Unlike the Challenge, its main function is deep development and technical mastery, not selection. It is an intensive one-week program with lectures, hands-on workshops and a closing hackathon.

21.1Purpose

Deepen the competencies for applied work in AI, agent engineering, data infrastructure, security, software systems and related technologies, through immersive instruction and hands-on execution.

21.3Advanced curriculum areas

The Bootcamp includes, at a minimum:

  1. Transformer architectures and advanced LLM fundamentals.
  2. Advanced agent engineering, planning and multi-agent orchestration.
  3. Advanced RAG systems, chunking, embeddings and vector databases.
  4. The MCP protocol and development of custom servers and tools.
  5. Systematic model evaluation, benchmarks and observability.
  6. AI security, data privacy, prompt injection mitigation and threat modeling.
  7. Infrastructure deployment, latency optimization, compute costs and sovereign AI.
  8. Open-source contribution workflows.
  9. Advanced software engineering and technical architecture.
  10. Product or domain integration, testing and production reliability.

21.4Delivery methodology

  • Instructor-led sessions.
  • Technical workshops.
  • Guided labs.
  • Architecture reviews.
  • Security reviews and model audits.
  • Mentoring.
  • Peer review.
  • Individual exercises.
  • Advanced project work.
  • Individual defense preparation.

21.5Mentoring model

Each participant has access to mentoring suited to their specialization during the modules and the hackathon.

  • Technical mentoring: AI system architecture, implementation decisions, infrastructure and deployment, testing and quality, security and data governance, integration of LLMs, RAG and MCP servers, and debugging.
  • Complementary or domain mentoring: problem framing, product reasoning, user research, business or operating models, economic analysis and cost viability, validation and strategic communication.

Mentor limits: they guide and give feedback, but do not do the participants' work, do not write material that participants present as their own and do not determine certification grades.

21.6Hackathon project

Teams choose among strategic project lines and split four functional roles, so that attribution and individual evaluation are clear.

The advanced project must demonstrate:

  • A clear problem definition.
  • Technical feasibility.
  • Working implementation.
  • Appropriate integration of AI, tools and data.
  • Documentation.
  • Testing and evaluation of results.
  • Security and privacy.
  • Validation.
  • Individual contribution.
  • Professional presentation.

21.7Bootcamp completion requirements

  1. Attendance at required modules and workshops.
  2. Assigned daily exercises.
  3. Active participation in the hackathon.
  4. Delivery of a working project repository with its documentation.
  5. Final group pitch and individual role defense.

Section 22Final evaluation

The final evaluation determines whether the participant has reached the competencies required for certification. It considers:

  • Technical or domain competence.
  • Final project quality.
  • Evidence of execution.
  • Individual authorship.
  • Understanding.
  • Technical or domain reasoning.
  • Validation.
  • Communication.
  • Final defense.

The evaluation philosophy is:

Result + Process + Understanding + Evidence + Defense

During the 15-minute group presentation on Day 6, the team explains why it built the solution, how it works, what assumptions it made, what evidence supports its decisions, what limitations remain and what each member contributed.

22.1Decision structure

The final evaluation separates three questions that are sometimes mixed up:

  1. Has the participant completed the required activities?
  2. Has the participant demonstrated the required competencies and authorship?
  3. Has the participant successfully defended the work before the designated evaluators?
