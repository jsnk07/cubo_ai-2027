Governance and risks

Who teaches, who scores, who decides and who certifies, and how the risks that could weaken the program are contained.

Section 19Governance, roles and decision rights

The program requires an explicit separation between instruction, mentoring, evaluation, selection, certification and digital verification.

19.2Decision rights

  • Mentors advise.
  • Instructors teach.
  • Evaluators score.
  • The Selection Committee decides progression.
  • The institutional authority certifies.
  • The verification provider supports digital verification.

These responsibilities must not be concentrated in a single role without documented oversight.

19.4Capacity planning

Capacity should be set before recruitment closes whenever possible, considering instructors, technical and complementary mentors, evaluators, compute infrastructure, facilities, budget, number of challenges and team size. The program should not recruit far beyond what it can support without lowering its training and evaluation standards.

19.5Mentoring operating model

During The AI Challenge, each team should have at least one technical and one complementary or domain mentoring session per week, subject to resources. During the Bootcamp, frequency increases with project complexity. Mentors guide, ask, review and give feedback; they do not do the participants' work or determine certification grades.

19.6Monitoring and key indicators (KPIs)

KPIs serve to improve the program's design and operation, not as isolated measures of participants' individual worth.
