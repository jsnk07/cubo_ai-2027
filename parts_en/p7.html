Part VII

Certification and verification

The credential is institutional and its verification is cryptographic. They are two different things and both matter.

Section 23Certification framework

CUBO_ai certification is based on demonstrated competence, not mere attendance. To qualify, the participant must:

  1. Complete the required curriculum.
  2. Complete The AI Challenge requirements.
  3. Advance successfully through the selection process.
  4. Complete the Technical Bootcamp.
  5. Complete the hackathon project.
  6. Reach the established competency thresholds.
  7. Successfully complete the final defense and evaluation.

Section 24Credential issuance and institutional governance

The certification architecture separates institutional certification from digital verification.

24.1 · CUBO_aiCurriculum design, training, evaluation, competency assessment and certification decision.
24.2 · ANIAThe National Artificial Intelligence Agency, the institution in charge of the program, officially issues and recognizes the credential, publishes it on the designated institutional platform and manages institutional records.
24.3 · Cryptographic verification layerSHA-256 hash and immutable timestamping (OpenTimestamps) associated with the credential's digital record.

24.3Cryptographic verification layer

The selected verification infrastructure uses cryptographic records, such as the SHA-256 hash and immutable timestamping with OpenTimestamps, associated with the digital credential record, to guarantee its integrity and traceability.

This layer must not be described as the institution that certifies participants. Its role is to provide cryptographic evidence associated with the digital record. The certificate remains an institutional credential issued by the competent authority.

Section 25Digital credential verification model

The proposed model is:

CUBO_ai evaluation → institutional certification → official credential issuance → publication → cryptographic timestamp → verification reference → independent verification

The operating principle is:

Through the designated official institutional portal, any third party can verify:

  1. Source file authenticity: the SHA-256 hash of the issued certificate PDF.
  2. Cryptographic timestamp: the record and the exact date and time the credential hash was anchored.
  3. Cryptographic proof: the verifiable proof that allows independent, decentralized validation.

Section 26Credential data specifications

A CUBO_ai digital credential may contain, where applicable:

  • Participant name.
  • Credential designation.
  • Program name.
  • Cohort or year.
  • Issue date.
  • Issuing institution (ANIA).
  • Competency framework.
  • Completion status.
  • Credential identifier.
  • Verification reference.
  • Cryptographic verification information, where applicable.

Publicly accessible records must minimize exposure of unnecessary personal information.
