Part III

The route and the Foundations

The program's phases, what it takes to move from one to the next and the five modules that form the common foundation.

Section 7Phase structure and program cycle

CUBO_ai is organized in sequential phases that make it possible to identify talent, establish a common knowledge base, assess capabilities by building solutions and develop advanced AI competencies. Each phase has specific objectives, progression requirements and verifiable results.

The path follows a competency-based progression logic:

The purpose is to ensure that participants not only understand AI fundamentals, but can apply them to solve real problems with working, documented and assessable solutions.

7.1Phase 1: Application and initial screening

Identifies candidates with learning potential, motivation, commitment and relevant capabilities to benefit from the training and contribute to AI projects. It is not limited to looking for prior programming experience: it seeks diverse profiles able to add value from different disciplines.

Evaluation dimensions
  • Academic or professional background: education, work experience, previous projects and other relevant evidence.
  • Technical or complementary experience: programming, data, research, product, design, business, operations or related areas.
  • Motivation and commitment: reasons for taking part, availability and willingness to complete activities and deliverables.
  • Analytical potential and problem solving: understanding complex situations, asking questions, identifying alternatives and justifying decisions.
  • Collaboration and communication: willingness to work in interdisciplinary teams, share knowledge and receive feedback.
  • Interest in applying AI: ability to recognize opportunities to use AI and their possible technical, operational or strategic impact.
  • Ability to complete the program: availability to meet the training, participation, evaluation and evidence requirements.

Initial screening seeks a diverse and complementary cohort, with participants able to benefit from a shared foundation and contribute to collective work from their individual strengths.

7.2Phase 2: Foundations and preparation

Sets the minimum level of knowledge and skills everyone must have before entering The AI Challenge. It is essential because the Challenge works as a practical assessment and selection mechanism, not as an introductory course. Preparation combines conceptual content, guided exercises and hands-on activities to build a common language between technical and strategic profiles.

Training components
  1. Artificial intelligence fundamentals. The evolution of AI, machine learning, neural networks, deep learning, LLMs, Transformer architectures, capabilities and limitations, ethics and responsible use.
  2. Programming and data management. Python, data structures, modularity, information handling and the basics of integrating with AI models and services. Non-technical profiles acquire what they need to understand the processes and collaborate with developers.
  3. AI tools and workflows. Prompt engineering, AI assistance tools, tool calling, APIs and basic agent concepts, with emphasis on validating results and human oversight.
  4. Collaboration and the CUBO_ai methodology. The program's working model, team dynamics, task planning, deliverable management and feedback.
  5. Repositories and version control. Git and GitHub, repository organization, commits, pull requests, contribution review and change management: the tools that distinguish individual contributions within collective work.
  6. Opportunity discovery. Researching needs, identifying real problems, understanding users, forming hypotheses and defining viable AI use cases.
  7. Documentation, evidence and authorship. Proposals, decision records, technical documentation and evidence of contribution; expectations on authorship, transparency in AI use, reproducibility and individual defense.

Progression criterion: at the end of this phase, the participant must show they understand what is needed to interpret a challenge, collaborate in an interdisciplinary team, use the program's basic tools and document their contributions.

7.3Phase 3: The AI Challenge

This is the central practical phase and the main applied assessment mechanism. For three weeks, interdisciplinary teams turn a scoped problem into a working prototype or an initial AI-based solution. It is detailed in Part IV.

Challenge objectives
  • Identify and understand a real problem and its users.
  • Define an AI application opportunity and set success criteria.
  • Design a solution and choose appropriate tools, models and approaches.
  • Build a working prototype, agent, automation or AI-assisted workflow.
  • Test how it works, document limitations and validate the results.
  • Keep evidence of the process and of individual contributions.
  • Present and defend the technical, operational and strategic decisions.
Minimum deliverables
  • Definition of the problem, users and opportunity.
  • Solution proposal and success criteria.
  • Working repository and evidence of contribution.
  • Working prototype or reproducible demo.
  • Documentation of the approach, tools and decisions.
  • Test results, limitations and possible improvements.
  • Final presentation and individual defense.

Collective performance does not replace individual evaluation: each participant must know their contributions and be able to explain the project's decisions, results and limitations.

7.4Phase 4: Technical Bootcamp and advanced specialization

For those who meet the Challenge selection criteria. It deepens the competencies needed to design, develop, evaluate and operate more complex AI systems, oriented to applied engineering and robust, integrable and scalable systems.

7.5Phase 5: Final project, evaluation and accreditation

Verifies that the participant can integrate what they learned into a technically grounded AI solution aimed at a concrete problem: design it, implement it, evaluate it, document it, justify their decisions and recognize its limitations.

  1. Product evaluation. Functionality, architecture, technical quality, testing, security, documentation and fit.
  2. Individual contribution audit. Commits, pull requests, change history, documentation and other evidence of each participant's contributions.
  3. Technical verification. How the system works, review of results and reproducibility of tests and demos.
  4. Individual defense before a panel. The problem, the solution, their responsibilities, the decisions, the evidence and the remaining limitations.
  5. Competency accreditation. Fulfillment of the approval criteria and, where applicable, issuance of institutional credentials.

7.6Cross-cutting implementation principles

  • Competency-based progression: each stage prepares for more complex responsibilities and challenges.
  • Interdisciplinarity: technical, strategic, analytical and operational contributions are all valued.
  • Hands-on learning: knowledge is applied in projects, experimentation and real problems.
  • Individual accountability: each participant demonstrates understanding and authorship, even when the result is a team's.
  • Responsible AI use: transparency, human validation and attention to limitations.
  • Evidence-based evaluation: progression and accreditation rest on deliverables, tests, documentation and defenses.
  • Traceability and transparency: evaluation processes, criteria and credentials have verifiable records.

Guiding principle: understand in order to create, build in order to validate, demonstrate in order to advance, and develop AI to solve real problems.

Section 8Foundations curriculum modules

The Foundations curriculum sets the conceptual and technical base everyone must master before entering The AI Challenge. There are five modules that rise from vision and structured thinking to agents and infrastructure.

F1

AI vision, problem analysis and structured thinking

Base
Purpose

Develop conceptual clarity about the impact of AI and analytical skills to break down complex problems, assessing technology application opportunities and their real limits.

Learning objectives
  • Understand why AI is transforming the global ecosystem and analyze the sector's opportunities.
  • Recognize intelligent systems in operational and everyday settings.
  • Diagnose problems through analytical decomposition, distinguishing symptoms from causes.
  • Assess common model failures and decide when the technology is appropriate.
  • Adopt a builder mindset rather than mere AI consumption.
Topics
  • F1.1Why AI and why now: the technological shift, the compute inflection point and current opportunities.
  • F1.2Recognizing intelligent systems around you: AI solutions in everyday and industrial processes.
  • F1.3Diagnosing model limits and failures: hallucinations, inaccurate answers and when to use AI or do without it.
  • F1.4Analytical decomposition and root cause: separating structural causes from surface symptoms.
  • F1.5The builder philosophy (Builders over Prompt Crafters): from superficial querying to understanding the architecture.
Practical deliverable

An analytical decomposition map of a real operational problem and an assessment of the reliability limits of the solution.

F2

Instruction design, verification and usage strategies

Use
Purpose

Master writing effective instructions, steering iterative AI workflows and the methods for auditing information, detecting bias and generating ideas.

Learning objectives
  • Structure clear instructions that define the expected output parameters.
  • Direct and iteratively correct the work done by AI.
  • Validate the accuracy of responses and detect bias in data.
  • Use AI as a catalyst for ideation and problem solving.
Topics
  • F2.1Writing effective instructions: prompt structure, defining the result upfront and steering the model iteratively.
  • F2.2Auditing responses and detecting bias: methods to verify information and neutralize bias in outputs.
  • F2.3Assisted ideation methods: structured workflows to innovate, generate ideas and compare alternatives with AI.
Practical deliverable

A repertoire of validated prompts and a data audit report on a case study.

F3

Ethics, data protection and responsible use policies

Use
Purpose

Analyze the developer's responsibility, address ethical dilemmas and set up internal frameworks to handle confidential information safely in AI environments.

Learning objectives
  • Assess ethical dilemmas, privacy and technological dependence in AI projects.
  • Handle sensitive and confidential data safely.
  • Design a responsible AI use guide or mini-policy for work teams.
Topics
  • F3.1Ethics, responsibility and technological dependence: bias, privacy and the responsibility of those who create AI.
  • F3.2Safeguarding confidential information and moral dilemmas: good practices with sensitive data and resolving operational dilemmas.
  • F3.3Human oversight (human-in-the-loop) and audit protocols: checkpoints, human validation before critical actions and traceability.
Practical deliverable

A human oversight protocol and a data audit matrix for an AI-assisted workflow.

F4

Data as a system, machine learning and computer vision

Models
Purpose

Understand the strategic importance of data quality, study the principles of machine learning and get started with visual processing using neural networks.

Learning objectives
  • Collect, clean and structure data, prioritizing its quality over architecture.
  • Understand from first principles how a machine learning model is trained and evaluated.
  • Analyze the structure of neural networks and image processing.
Topics
  • F4.1Data as a system: capture, cleaning and organization; why data quality decides an AI project.
  • F4.2Machine learning from first principles: essential concepts and building predictive models step by step.
  • F4.3Neural networks and computer vision: neural network fundamentals and image and video processing.
Practical deliverable

A data preparation pipeline and a technical report evaluating a basic predictive or visual perception model.

F5

Language models, agents, the MCP protocol and infrastructure

Systems
Purpose

Master modern LLMs, integrating external sources through RAG and agents, applying the MCP protocol and analyzing infrastructure and sovereign AI.

Learning objectives
  • Explain how LLMs work, their capabilities and their limits.
  • Build applications with RAG systems and agents.
  • Implement the Model Context Protocol (MCP) to connect models with tools and data sources.
  • Apply systems thinking to design reliable and scalable solutions.
  • Analyze physical infrastructure, compute costs and technological sovereignty.
Topics
  • F5.1Understanding LLMs: architecture, capabilities and limits of modern models.
  • F5.2RAG systems and agents: vector databases, tools and simple agents that carry out tasks.
  • F5.3Model Context Protocol (MCP) and skills: secure access to data sources and custom skills.
  • F5.4Systems thinking and scalability: reliable AI architectures in production.
  • F5.5Infrastructure, compute economics and sovereign AI: hardware, costs, energy use and sovereign deployment.
Practical deliverable

A working prototype of an agent with RAG and an integrated MCP server, with an architecture diagram and an assessment of infrastructure costs.

The Student Guide develops each module with key ideas, questions to think about, self-assessment, readings and a hands-on exercise (DIY) with a technical and a complementary route.

8.1Readiness Standard

The Foundations phase ensures a shared technical language and the minimum readiness level so that each participant can contribute actively from their specialization. It does not require everyone to reach the same technical mastery.

8.1.2 Technical entry expectations

Anyone entering through the technical route must be able, at a minimum, to:

  1. Read, write and debug basic Python code.
  2. Work comfortably in the command line.
  3. Manage repositories with Git and GitHub following clean collaborative workflows.
  4. Understand the structure of an API, the JSON format and calls to external services.
8.1.3 Readiness Gate

Before The AI Challenge starts, the academic team validates completion of the Foundations sessions and hands-on workshops. Anyone with technical gaps receives a structured reinforcement plan, so that no team starts practical work without the required preparation.
