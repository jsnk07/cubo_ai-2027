Part II

Profiles and competencies

Who takes part, from which specialization they contribute and what everyone has to demonstrate, whatever their profile.

Section 4Profiles and specialization routes

CUBO_ai recognizes two broad profiles. Both work on the same problem and share the same foundation, but each is evaluated on the responsibilities that belong to it.

4.1Technical profiles

  • Implementation of software and services.
  • Integration of language models and APIs.
  • Design of agents, tools and logical flows.
  • Environment setup, testing and deployment.
  • Repository management, version control and technical documentation.
  • Assessment of errors, security and performance.

4.2Complementary profiles

  • User research and context analysis.
  • Definition and prioritization of needs.
  • Curation of data, test cases and examples.
  • Design of instructions and criteria for evaluating responses.
  • Risk, ethics and governance analysis.
  • Strategic documentation, value proposition and operational viability.

Complementary participants are not “half developers”. Their value lies in their domain expertise, their ability to understand the problem and their skill at turning real needs into requirements, test data and criteria for judging whether an AI system works.

Section 5Shared competency framework

Whatever their profile, all participants develop core competencies assessed with verifiable evidence. Each participant must show that they:

  • Understand the purpose of the solution.
  • Can describe the path the data takes.
  • Recognize the model's limits.
  • Interpret the results.
  • Explain how correct operation was verified.

Section 6Opportunity discovery framework

CUBO_ai uses a common methodological cycle so that technical and complementary profiles approach problems systematically.

6.1Detect

Observe processes, services or needs where there is friction, unnecessary cost, delay or information that is hard to use.

  • What is happening and who does it affect?
  • Where does the friction occur?
  • What evidence shows the problem exists?

6.2Understand

Research the users, the operating context, the constraints, the risks and the solutions that already exist.

  • Users and stakeholders.
  • Current processes.
  • Existing solutions.
  • Available data and its quality.
  • Technical or operational constraints.
  • Privacy and bias risks.

6.3Scope

Turn the opportunity into a problem statement with clear users, scope, objective and success criteria. Technical profiles translate it into system requirements, architecture, APIs and data flows; complementary profiles, into user requirements, test cases, response-quality criteria and value assumptions.

6.4Build or prototype

Design a response proportionate to the time available. Depending on the challenge, it may be:

  • An automated workflow.
  • An assistant or agent.
  • A classification system.
  • A simulation.
  • A RAG system.
  • An MCP server.
  • A low-code/no-code prototype.
  • An AI-assisted prototype.

6.5Validate

Test the solution with defined cases, log errors, compare results and explain what evidence supports the conclusions. In an AI system this includes measuring how often it is right, in which cases it fails and how serious each failure is.
