Section 29Exceptions, remediation and re-evaluation

A complete framework must define what happens when participants, teams, mentors or evaluators face situations outside the normal flow.

29.1Participant remediation

May be authorized when a participant does not pass a Foundations readiness activity or another explicitly defined checkpoint. Remediation must have:

  • A defined learning objective.
  • A defined deadline.
  • A responsible instructor or mentor.
  • A documented completion criterion.

29.2Technical or infrastructure failure

If a participant or team cannot demonstrate a required component because of a documented infrastructure or external AI service failure beyond their reasonable control, the panel may evaluate the available evidence and use an approved fallback procedure.

The fallback does not automatically turn an incomplete implementation into a passing result: it preserves the distinction between demonstrated, simulated and proposed functionality.

29.3Exceptional circumstances

The program may set a procedure for force-majeure events that materially prevent a participant from meeting a deadline. Any exception must be documented and approved by the designated authority.

29.4Re-evaluation

Only allowed under the approved rules. It must not be used to resubmit a weak project over and over until the desired result is reached.

29.5Appeals

Appeals address procedural or scoring problems backed by the evaluation record, with a deadline, a reviewing authority, required evidence and a final decision date.

Section 30Final program architecture

CUBO_ai is structured as a selective applied-learning route:

Section 31Strategic program principle

CUBO_ai does not seek to produce participants with identical profiles. Its goal is to develop professionals who operate effectively within a technology ecosystem while keeping a meaningful specialization.

Same Problem. Different Specialization. Shared Execution Standard.“Mismo problema. Distinta especialización. Un mismo estándar de ejecución.”

Developers learn to identify opportunities that technology can address and to build the corresponding solutions.

Complementary participants learn to identify and analyze opportunities from their disciplines, translate them into technology requirements, collaborate with technical participants and use appropriate tools to prototype and validate solutions.

Both profiles must:

  • Understand the problem.
  • Contribute meaningfully.
  • Provide evidence.
  • Defend their work.

Section 32Final institutional position

CUBO_ai establishes a structured bridge between education and practical participation in the Artificial Intelligence ecosystem, digital transformation and sovereign technology infrastructure. Its model integrates:

  • Foundational education.
  • Interdisciplinary specialization.
  • Opportunity discovery.
  • Practical execution.
  • Evidence-based evaluation.
  • Individual authorship.
  • Responsible AI use.
  • Advanced technical development.
  • Real-world case studies.
  • Institutional certification.
  • Digital credential verification.

The central goal is a repeatable institutional path: moving from foundational knowledge to applied execution, demonstrating individual competence with auditable evidence, building advanced capabilities in the Technical Bootcamp and receiving a credential whose institutional origin and digital verification process can be examined independently.

This framework is the operating architecture of CUBO_ai. The annual implementation plan, the Project Catalog, detailed course materials, mentor assignments, schedules and the technical implementation of verification provide the detail each cohort needs, and changes to them must remain consistent with the principles, decision rights, evaluation standards and progression logic of this document.
