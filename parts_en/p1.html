Part I

Vision and principles

What CUBO_ai is, who it trains and the rules that hold the whole program together.

Section 1Executive summary

CUBO_ai is a structured talent development program that introduces participants to Artificial Intelligence, builds practical construction skills and assesses their ability to turn AI concepts into working technology solutions.

The program moves from foundational understanding to practical development, with emphasis on prompt engineering, tool calling, logical control flows, AI agents, machine learning, large language models (LLMs), AI infrastructure and systems thinking.

Participants arrive with different technical, academic and professional backgrounds. That is why CUBO_ai does not force non-technical profiles into traditional software roles, nor limits developers to writing code. The framework first establishes a solid common base in Artificial Intelligence and open-source principles. Then each participant contributes from their own specialization.

Participants are expected not only to interact with AI models, but to understand their capabilities and limitations, integrate tools and data sources, design logical workflows, build working prototypes and defend the technical and strategic decisions behind their solutions.

  • Application and screening: assessment of background, commitment, analytical potential, learning ability and problem solving.
  • Foundations and preparation: introduction to AI and the evolution of its paradigms, ethics, data, Python, programming, data structures and essential language-model concepts.
  • Opportunity discovery: identifying real problems, user needs and AI application opportunities through research, analysis and use-case definition.
  • The AI Challenge: an intensive three-week challenge in which interdisciplinary teams design, build, test, document and defend a working AI-based prototype, applying Python, LLMs, prompt engineering, APIs, agents and tool integration.
  • Evaluation and final selection: assessment of technical performance, functionality, architecture, testing, individual contribution, collaboration and oral defense, with a reference threshold of 70/100 and subject to available capacity.
  • Technical Bootcamp and advanced specialization: training in agent engineering, Transformer architectures, RAG systems, vector databases, MCP, automation, model evaluation, observability, scalability and AI infrastructure.
  • Final project, evaluation and defense: a complete AI solution backed by code, technical documentation, reproducible tests and verifiable evidence of individual contributions.
  • Institutional certification: accreditation of the competencies achieved, according to the program's academic, technical and institutional criteria.
  • Official publication: recording of credentials on the corresponding institutional portal or official platform.
  • Digital verification: independent verification of credentials through digital identifiers, verifiable records and timestamps.

1.1Methodological approach

The model combines progressive technical training with project-based learning, real challenges and competency-based assessment. Participants move from AI fundamentals to building applied systems, integrating technical knowledge, analytical thinking, interdisciplinary collaboration and communication.

The AI Challenge is the program's main practical filter. It is not a passive introductory course, but an intensive build-and-validate experience: participants identify opportunities, analyze needs, design architectures, develop prototypes, run tests, document decisions and defend their results. Selection for the next stage is based on performance evidence and competency criteria defined in advance.

Evaluation stays traceable and transparent across three complementary dimensions:

  • Product evaluation: functionality, architecture quality, testing, security and fit of the solution.
  • Individual contribution audit: GitHub change history, code reviews, documentation and evidence of participation.
  • Technical defense: justification of decisions, demonstration of how it works and answers to the panel's questions.

Those who meet the criteria advance to specialization in more complex AI systems: autonomous agents, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), tool integration, observability and scalable infrastructure.

1.2Expected outcome and transparent verification

CUBO_ai aims for professionals to turn concrete problems into working, assessable and sustainable AI solutions, prioritizing demonstrable results, verifiable contributions and solutions aligned with real needs.

The program is governed by the principle of transparent verification and evidence-based evaluation, at two levels:

  1. Participant-level verification: individual competencies, authorship of code and documentation, problem solving and technical execution, backed by Git logs, pull requests, review histories, functional tests and live oral defenses.
  2. Credential- and system-level verification: integrity, provenance and traceability of solutions and issued credentials, with recording and digital verification mechanisms that allow their authenticity to be checked.

Guiding principle: learn by building, prove through evidence and advance by competency.

Section 2Purpose and design principles

2.1Program purpose

The main goal of CUBO_ai is to train high-caliber professional talent able to drive innovation, security and infrastructure resilience within the global Artificial Intelligence ecosystem and El Salvador's emerging digital economy.

The program connects academic learning with practical execution through four complementary dimensions:

Artificial Intelligence and digital infrastructure literacy

Participants develop an understanding of:

  • AI fundamentals and machine learning architectures.
  • Distributed systems and compute networks for AI.
  • Deep learning, neural networks and LLMs.
  • Open-source software and open models.
  • Agent architecture and tool integration.
  • Data infrastructure, vector databases and MCP servers.
  • Compute infrastructure and sovereign AI.
  • Digital security, bias and operational risk.
Opportunity discovery

Participants learn to identify problems and opportunities from different professional perspectives. Technical participants focus on opportunities that software, infrastructure, automation, data or technical systems can address. Complementary participants can analyze them from disciplines such as:

  • Business
  • Economics
  • Finance
  • Product
  • Design
  • Research
  • Operations
  • Communications
  • Marketing
  • Strategy
  • Other relevant disciplines
Applied execution

Participants must turn an identified opportunity into a concrete result. Depending on their profile, this may involve:

  • Software development.
  • Technical architecture.
  • Product design.
  • Research.
  • Business analysis.
  • Financial modeling.
  • User research.
  • Operational design.
  • Prototyping.
  • AI-assisted development.
  • Low-code/no-code experimentation.
  • Validation.
Professional defense

Participants must be able to explain:

  • What problem they identified and why it matters.
  • Who experiences it.
  • What solution they propose and why it is technically or operationally viable.
  • What assumptions they made and what evidence supports their decisions.
  • What their individual contribution was.
  • What limitations remain.

2.2Design principles

These principles govern the whole CUBO_ai operating model. They are not isolated statements: each one is reflected in the curriculum, participant roles, The AI Challenge, the evaluation framework, the Bootcamp, certification and the governance model.

2.2.1 Shared foundation

All participants receive a common foundation before specialization and practical selection. This creates a shared vocabulary about AI, open-source collaboration, opportunity discovery, documentation, evidence and responsible use of technology. The minimum readiness standard is defined in section 8.1.

2.2.2 Specialized contribution

Each participant is evaluated according to the responsibilities of their profile: technical participants for their execution and technical reasoning; complementary participants for their research, problem definition, product or domain analysis, validation and technology translation. The model is detailed in Section 4 and put into practice during The AI Challenge in Section 10.

2.2.3 Interdisciplinary execution

Technical and complementary profiles work on the same problem with different responsibilities. The goal is not to make them interchangeable, but to reproduce a realistic multidisciplinary work environment.

2.2.4 Evidence-based evaluation

Evaluation considers the product, the process, understanding, evidence and defense. A completed artifact is not enough if the participant cannot explain it, and a high volume of activity is not enough if it does not show meaningful progress.

2.2.5 Individual accountability

Belonging to a team does not replace individual evaluation. Each participant must show authorship, understanding and a meaningful contribution. This separation is formalized in the 60% team + 40% individual model of Section 14.

2.2.6 Responsible AI use

AI tools can support research, development, analysis, documentation, prototyping and automation, but participants remain responsible for verifying and defending the result. The program evaluates the participant's understanding and accountability, not whether they used AI.

2.2.7 Progressive difficulty

CUBO_ai does not require advanced technical mastery at entry. The Foundations set the common minimum level; The AI Challenge assesses readiness; the Technical Bootcamp builds deeper competence; and the advanced project provides evidence of applied mastery.

2.2.8 Institutional traceability

Important program decisions must be backed by documented criteria, evidence, responsible authorities and defined procedures.

Section 3Team model and mixed cohorts

CUBO_ai promotes teams in which engineering profiles and analysis, business or research profiles complement each other.

  • Technical profile programming and service integration, architecture design and system logic, connecting models, tools and APIs, testing, debugging and deployment.
  • Strategic / complementary profile problem definition and user analysis, research, context and assumption validation, workflow design and quality criteria, documentation, value proposition and adoption strategy.

Teams usually have four participants: two technical and two complementary. The division of roles does not create isolated silos: each person must understand how their work connects with the whole solution.

The purpose of this structure is to create a realistic environment in which specialists collaborate to solve a shared problem.
