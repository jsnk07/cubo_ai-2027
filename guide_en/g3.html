F3.1Ethics, responsibility and technological dependence

An AI system makes or influences decisions at a scale no person can reach. If it has a bias, it repeats it thousands of times. That is why ethics is not a separate chapter of the project: it is in every design decision.

Four questions you should be able to answer about any system you build:

  • Fairness: does it treat groups of people differently without a legitimate reason?
  • Privacy: does it use personal data it doesn't need or that people did not authorize?
  • Transparency: do people know they are interacting with AI and can they understand why a decision was made?
  • Accountability: if it is wrong, who answers for it and how is it corrected?

There is also the risk of dependence: depending on a single provider, on a model that can change without notice or on a tool the team no longer knows how to replace. Designing with alternatives, and with open models where possible, reduces that risk; it is part of what is called sovereign AI (F5.5).

Key ideas
  • Fairness, privacy, transparency and accountability: four questions for every system.
  • Technological dependence is also a risk you design for.
Think about itIf your system wrongly denies a service, how does the person find out and whom can they complain to?

F3.2Safeguarding confidential information and moral dilemmas

Before sending data to a model, ask where it goes. If you use a cloud service, the text leaves your computer and is subject to that provider's terms. If you use a local model, like those in Ollama, it stays on your machine.

Basic good practices:

  • Minimize: send only the data the task needs.
  • Anonymize or pseudonymize: replace names, ID numbers, phone numbers and emails with placeholders before processing.
  • Classify: public, internal, confidential and restricted, each level with rules on which tool can touch it.
  • Log: what data was used, for what and with which tool.
  • Comply: check the applicable data protection law and your institution's policies.

Real dilemmas are rarely black and white: a more accurate system may need more personal data; a more useful answer may expose a third party's information. Documenting how you decided is as important as the decision.

Key ideas
  • Knowing where the data goes is the first security decision.
  • Minimize and anonymize before processing.
Think about itWhich data from your work would you never paste into a cloud assistant? Why?

F3.3Human oversight and audit protocols

Human-in-the-loop means a person reviews or approves at a defined point in the workflow. It is not reviewing “when there is time”: it is a designed control, with clear criteria.

To design it, answer:

  1. Which actions are critical? Sending a message to a citizen, approving a payment, deleting data, publishing something.
  2. Who approves and with what information? The person must see the system's proposal and its basis, not just a button.
  3. What happens if they reject it? The case goes back to a queue, is corrected or escalated.
  4. What is recorded? Input, model output, human decision, who and when. That is traceability.

A good protocol also defines thresholds: for example, the system acts on its own only when its confidence is high and the risk low, and asks for review in everything else.

Key ideas
  • Human oversight is designed at specific points in the workflow.
  • Without a record there is no possible audit.
Think about itIf the human reviewer approves 99% without reading, is there still oversight? How would you prevent it?

F3 · Self-assessmentCheck what you learned

What are the four basic ethical questions for an AI system?

Fairness, privacy, transparency and accountability.

In terms of privacy, what is the difference between a local model and a cloud one?

With a local model the data does not leave your machine; with a cloud service it travels to the provider and is subject to its terms.

What does pseudonymize mean?

Replacing the data that identifies a person with placeholders or codes, so the text stays useful without exposing them.

What must a workflow with human oversight record?

The input, the model's proposal, the human decision, who made it and when.

Why is dependence on a provider an ethical and operational risk?

Because the service, the model or its terms can change outside the institution's control, affecting continuity, costs and data.

DIY WORKSHOP · F3

Protected data and a human in the loop

Take an AI-assisted workflow, protect the data before it reaches the model and define where a person approves. Produces the F3 deliverable.

3 hoursIndividual or in pairsTechnical: Python + Ollama
Technical route
Personal data filter and approval point
  1. Create cuboai-f3 with five made-up sample messages that include fake names, emails and phone numbers.
  2. Write redact.py with regular expressions that replace emails and phone numbers with [EMAIL] and [PHONE]: 
    import re
def redact(t):
    t = re.sub(r"[\w.+-]+@[\w-]+\.[\w.]+", "[EMAIL]", t)
    t = re.sub(r"\b\d{4}[- ]?\d{4}\b", "[PHONE]", t)
    return t
  3. Send the redacted text to your local model so it proposes a reply.
  4. Before “sending”, show the proposal and ask for approval with input("Approve? (y/n) "). If not, the case stays pending.
  5. Save each decision to audit.jsonl: time, redacted text, proposal, decision and reviewer.
  6. Document in the README which personal data still slips past your filter (for example, names) and how you would fix it.
Complementary route
Mini-policy, data matrix and protocol
  1. Choose a real workflow that could use AI, for example answering users' queries about a service.
  2. Build the data audit matrix: each data item that comes in, its sensitivity level, whether it is necessary, which tool can process it and how long it is kept.
  3. Draw the workflow and mark the points where a person reviews or approves, with their decision criterion.
  4. Write a one-page mini-policy: which tools are allowed, which data is never used, how AI use is disclosed and to whom incidents are reported.
  5. Ask a classmate to find a gap in your policy and fix it.
Deliverable

A human oversight protocol (checkpoints, owners, criteria and logging) and a data audit matrix for an AI-assisted workflow.

Checklist
  • Each data item has a sensitivity level and a rule.
  • Critical actions have a human approval point.
  • Every decision is logged.
  • You acknowledge at least one limitation of your solution.
