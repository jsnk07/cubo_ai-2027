Before you start

How to use this guide

Your map to study the Foundations, prepare for The AI Challenge and arrive ready at the Technical Bootcamp.

WelcomeWhat you will find here

This guide accompanies every CUBO_ai class. It explains the topics of the five Foundations modules in study-friendly language, suggests exercises to practice alone or in a team and prepares you for the two stages where your progress is decided: The AI Challenge and the Technical Bootcamp.

It does not replace classes or instructors. Use it before each session to arrive with context, and afterwards to review and practice. Every module has the same structure:

ObjectivesWhat you should be able to do when you finish the module. Use them as a checklist.
TopicsOne reading per topic, with key ideas and a question to think about.
Self-assessmentQuestions with answers to check you understood before moving on.
DIY workshopA hands-on exercise with two routes, technical and complementary, that produces the module's deliverable.

01The two DIY routes

CUBO_ai works with a single cohort and different profiles. That is why each DIY has two routes that reach the same learning by different paths:

  • Technical route: Python, the terminal, local models, APIs and MCP servers. For development profiles.
  • Complementary route: research, analysis, test cases, quality criteria and documents. For business, product, design, data, law or operations profiles.

You can do both. Trying the route that is not yours is the best way to understand your future teammates. Each DIY gives its estimated time, what you need, the steps, the deliverable and a checklist.

02Three rules for everything you hand in

Leave evidenceWork in a repository or document with history. Small, frequent commits count more than a bulk upload on the last day.
Everything you deliver, you defendIf you cannot explain something with your name on it, it does not count as yours. That is how the individual defense works.
Use AI responsiblyYou can use it to research, code or write, but you verify, test and answer for the result.
Builders over prompt craftersAsking a model is not enough: you have to understand, build and verify.

ToolkitSet up your environment

Install this during the first week. Everything is free. The language models in the technical exercises run on your own computer inside Docker, with Ollama: you don't need to pay for an API or send data to third parties. Complementary profiles only need a browser, an AI assistant and a spreadsheet.

Never use real personal data in the exercises. Use public, synthetic or anonymized data. Ollama runs on your machine, but cloud assistants do not: whatever you paste there leaves your computer. Learning to decide which data can go where is part of module F3.

If your computer has little memory, use the small models the DIYs suggest (for example, llama3.2:1b). They are slower and make more mistakes, and that also helps you learn about model limits.
